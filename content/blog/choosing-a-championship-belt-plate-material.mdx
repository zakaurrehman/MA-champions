Most people choosing a championship belt start with the design. It is usually
the wrong place to start. The **metal** decides more about how the finished belt
looks and feels than the artwork does, and it is the single biggest factor in
what you pay.

Here is what actually separates the three.

## Brass

Brass is the entry point. It is soft enough to etch cleanly, light enough to
wear comfortably over a shoulder for a long night, and the cheapest of the
three to work.

The trade-off is depth. A standard etch on brass produces detail you can see
clearly at arm's length but which flattens out under close inspection. For a
gift, a first belt, or a design that is mostly lettering and flat shapes, that
is completely fine.

**Choose brass if** you want a real metal belt on a real leather strap without
paying for detail you will not notice.

## Zinc

Zinc is die-cast rather than cut, and it holds a much deeper etch than brass.
That extra depth is what lets a plate throw a genuine shadow, and shadow is
what makes relief read as three-dimensional rather than printed.

This is where most serious replica work sits. If you have ever picked up two
belts and found one obviously sharper without being able to say why, the
difference was usually etch depth.

**Choose zinc if** the design has fine detail — scrollwork, lettering with
serifs, textured fields — and you want it to survive close inspection.

## CNC and HD

CNC plates are machined out of solid stock rather than cast from a mould. There
is no mould to soften the edges, so the cuts come out crisp and the recesses
come out deep. It is the closest you get to a screen-accurate replica.

It is also the heaviest and the most expensive, for the obvious reason: you are
paying for machine time and for the metal that gets cut away and thrown out.

**Choose CNC if** accuracy is the point — a display piece, a collector's belt,
or a flagship award where somebody will absolutely pick it up and study it.

## What about the plating?

Plating and base metal are separate decisions, and it is worth understanding
why they interact.

Genuine 24k gold plating is applied over a polished base. That polish is doing
real work: plate over an unpolished surface and the gold sits on top of every
scratch underneath, so the finish looks flat however much gold you use. This is
the difference between a plated belt that keeps its depth and one that looks
tired within a year.

Beware anything described as "gold coloured" or "gold tone" without the word
*plated*. That is usually lacquer, and lacquer chips.

## The short version

| If you want | Choose |
| --- | --- |
| The lowest price on a real metal belt | Brass |
| Sharp detail at a sensible price | Zinc |
| Maximum accuracy and weight | CNC / HD |
